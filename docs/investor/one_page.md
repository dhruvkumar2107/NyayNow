# NyayNow — one-page investor brief

**Thesis.** Build a source-first legal workflow and intelligence layer for India: retrieve applicable legal material, preserve passage-level provenance and effective dates, provide multilingual interaction, and make uncertainty visible. The defensible asset should be a lawfully maintained corpus, temporal legal graph, citation verifier, and expert-reviewed evaluation loop—not a generic model wrapper.

**Problem context.** The official National Judicial Data Grid district-court dashboard displayed 51,591,381 pending cases on 17 October 2026. This is a changing district-court snapshot, not a market-size estimate or addressable-user count. India Code records BNS commencement on 1 July 2024; NyayNow has not yet built a counsel-reviewed transition map.

**Product today.** The repository contains a Next.js client and Express API, Gemini-based generation, optional Indian Kanoon search in selected routes, account/payment flows, and legal workflow UI. It does not establish comprehensive court coverage, custom embedding retrieval, proprietary base models, validated legal accuracy, live advocate verification, or production citation receipts. Outcome prediction is disabled by default.

**Deep-tech wedge.** Version legal sources with jurisdiction, court, effective dates, licence, and immutable passage spans. Retrieve hybrid lexical/semantic evidence; validate source identity, passage match, currency, and supported propositions; abstain when evidence is insufficient. Measure citation existence, passage support, stale-law errors, abstention, multilingual quality, human correction time, latency, and cost. Exact quote checks alone do not prove entailment.

**Business model hypothesis.** Public payment configuration contains monthly prices of ₹299, ₹799, and ₹2,999 for tiers. Entitlements and commercial terms require reconciliation; no willingness-to-pay, revenue, retention, or unit-economics claim is supported in this brief.

**Milestones.** (1) source rights and counsel review; (2) permissioned corpus and temporal schema; (3) baseline retrieval/citation evaluation; (4) consented design-partner pilots; (5) paid conversion and measured unit economics; (6) independent evaluation and scale plan.
